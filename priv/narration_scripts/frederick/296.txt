[reverent, measured] I look at Thee, my Lord Jesus, and think of Thy Most Holy Body, and I keep it before me as the pledge of my own resurrection. [short pause] [quiet, steady] Though I die, as die I certainly shall, nevertheless I shall not for ever die, for I shall rise again. [pause] [thoughtful] My Lord, the heathen who knew Thee not, thought the body to be of a miserable and contemptible nature — they thought it the seat, the cause, the excuse of all moral evil. When their thoughts soared highest, and they thought of a future life, they considered that the destruction of the body was the condition of that higher existence. That the body was really part of themselves, and that its restoration could be a privilege, was beyond their utmost imagination. [long pause] [wondering] And indeed, what mind of man, O Lord, could ever have fancied without Thy revelation that what, according to our experience, is so vile, so degraded, so animal, so sinful, which is our fellowship with the brutes, which is full of corruption and becomes dust and ashes, was in its very nature capable of so high a destiny! that it could become celestial and immortal, without ceasing to be a body! [short pause] [softly, with awe] And who but Thou, who art omnipotent, could have made it so! [short pause] [measured] No wonder then, that the wise men of the world, who did not believe in Thee, scoffed at the Resurrection. [long pause] [warm, heartfelt] But I, by Thy grace, will ever keep before me how differently I have been taught by Thee. [short pause] [with quiet conviction] O best and first and truest of Teachers! O Thou who art the Truth, I know, and believe with my whole heart, that this very flesh of mine will rise again. I know, base and odious as it is at present, that it will one day, if I be worthy, be raised incorruptible and altogether beautiful and glorious. [pause] [softly, resolved] This I know; this, by Thy grace, I will ever keep before me.
