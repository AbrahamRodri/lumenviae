[solemn, measured] Our Lord spent 30 years of His life obeying, three years teaching, three hours redeeming! [short pause] But how did He redeem? [short pause] Suppose a golden chalice is stolen from an altar and beaten into a large ash tray. Before that gold can be returned to the altar, it must be thrown into a fire, where the dross is burned away; then the chalice must be recast, and finally blessed and restored to its holy use. [long pause] [grave, thoughtful] Sinful man is like that chalice which was delivered over to profane uses. [short pause] He lost his Godlike resemblance and his high destiny as a child of God. [short pause] [low, reverent] So our blessed Lord took unto Himself a human nature, making it stand for all of us, plunged it into the fires of Calvary to have the dross of sin burned and purged away. [short pause] [brighter] Then, by rising from the dead, He became the new head of the new humanity, according to which we are all to be patterned. [long pause] [earnest, rising] The cross reveals that unless there is a Good Friday in our lives, there will never be an Easter Sunday. [short pause] Unless there is a crown of thorns, there will never be the halo of light. Unless there is the scourged body, there will never be a glorified one. [pause] [quiet, firm] Death to the lower self is the condition of resurrection to the higher self. [long pause] [tender, heartfelt] The world says to us, as it said to Him on the cross: “Come down, and we will believe!” [short pause] But if He had come down, He never would have saved us. [short pause] [slowly] It is human to come down; it is divine to hang there. [pause] [prayerful, heartfelt] A broken heart, O Saviour of the world, is love’s best cradle! Smite my own, as Moses did the rock, that Thy love may enter in!
