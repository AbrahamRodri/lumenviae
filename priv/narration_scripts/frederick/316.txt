[calm, measured] Christ wished to be baptized in order to consecrate the baptism wherewith we were to be baptized. And therefore it behooved those things to be shown forth which belong to the efficacy of our baptism: concerning which efficacy three points are to be considered. [short pause] First, the principal power from which it is derived; and this, indeed, is a heavenly power. For which reason, [short pause] when Christ was baptized, heaven was opened, to show that in future the heavenly power would sanctify baptism. [long pause] [thoughtful] Secondly, the faith of the Church and of the person baptized conduces to the efficacy of baptism: wherefore those who are baptized make a profession of faith, and baptism is called the "sacrament of faith." Now by faith we gaze on heavenly things, [short pause] which surpass the senses and human reason. And in order to signify this, the heavens were opened when Christ was baptized. [long pause] [warm, with quiet conviction] Thirdly, because the entrance to the heavenly kingdom was opened to us by the baptism of Christ in a special manner, which entrance had been closed to the first man through sin. Hence, when Christ was baptized, [short pause] the heavens were opened, to show that the way to heaven is open to the baptized.
