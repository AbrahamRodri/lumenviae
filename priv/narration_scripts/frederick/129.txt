[calm, measured] Any cross would be easy to bear if we could only tailor it to fit ourselves. [short pause] Our Lord’s cross was not made by Him, but for Him. Crosses and burdens are thrust upon us. [short pause] Our acceptance makes them personal. Our Lord even said that there would be at least seven crosses a week: [short pause] “Take up your cross daily and follow Me.” [long pause] [thoughtful] Crosses are of two kinds: pure ones, which come from the outside, such as pain, persecution, and ridicule; and inner, or impure crosses, which come as the result of our sins, such as sadness, despair, and unhappiness. [short pause] These latter crosses can be avoided. They are made by contradicting the will of God. [long pause] [deliberate] The vertical bar of the cross stands for God’s will; the horizontal bar stands for our wills. [short pause] When one crosses the other, we have the cross. [pause] Our Lord never promised that we would be without a cross; He only promised that we would never be overcome by one. [long pause] [tender, heartfelt] St. Peter so loved the cross that when the time came for his execution he asked to be crucified upside down. [short pause] [prayerful] May He who was found guilty of no other crime than that of the excess of love make us hate the load of sin that made His cross. [short pause] The whole cross borne in union with His will and following in His footsteps is easier to bear than the splinters against which we rebel.
