[calm, measured] God enables man to work miracles for two reasons. [short pause] First and principally, in confirmation of the doctrine that a man teaches. For since those things which are of faith surpass human reason, they cannot be proved by human arguments, but need to be proved by the argument of Divine power: so that when a man does works that God alone can do, we may believe that what he says is from God: [short pause] just as when a man is the bearer of letters sealed with the king's ring, it is to be believed that what they contain expresses the king's will. [long pause] [thoughtful] Secondly, in order to make known God's presence in a man by the grace of the Holy Ghost: so that when a man does the works of God we may believe that God dwells in him by His grace. [short pause] Wherefore it is written: "He who giveth to you the Spirit, and worketh miracles among you." [long pause] [reverent, with conviction] Now both these things were to be made known to men concerning Christ—namely, that God dwelt in Him by grace, not of adoption, but of union: and that His supernatural doctrine was from God. And therefore it was most fitting [short pause] that He should work miracles.
