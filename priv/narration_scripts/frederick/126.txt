[solemn, measured] As a kind person in the face of pain seeks to relieve the sufferings of his friend, so does moral kindness in the face of evil take on the punishment which evil deserves. [short pause] Every mother would willingly, if she could, bear the aches of her child. A father will pay the debts of his wayward son as if they were his own. [short pause] [sorrowful] Our Lord, though guilty of no sin, nevertheless in His agony in the garden permitted Himself to feel the inner effects of sin, as on the cross He experienced also the external effects of sin. These internal effects were sadness, fear, and a sense of loneliness. [pause] [grieved, quiet] “I looked for one that would grieve together with Me, and I found none." [long pause] [low, grave] He permits His head to feel blasphemies as if His lips had pronounced them; His hands to feel the sins of theft, as if He had stolen; His body to sense the guilt of defilement, as if it were the cause. [short pause] [slower] Innocence knows sin better than the guilty, because the guilty are already part of it. [long pause] [grave, deliberate] Sin is in the blood. The drunkard, the libertine, the tyrant have registered sin not only in their souls, but in their brains, the cells of their body, and the very expressions of their faces. [short pause] If, therefore, sin is in the blood, to atone for it, blood must be poured out. Our Lord never intended that any other blood than His own should be shed in expiation for sins. [short pause] [heavier] Because men have not invoked the blood of Christ for their sins, they are now at war shedding one another’s blood. [long pause] [firm, quietly confident] The agony in the garden is not a triumph of the plans and the schemes of betrayers and enemies, but is permitted by divine decree. [short pause] “This is your hour,” our Lord said to His enemies. [pause] [with quiet strength] Evil has its hour, but God has His day!
