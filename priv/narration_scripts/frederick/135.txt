[tender, storytelling] Our Lady went into a strange country, Our Lady, for she was ours [short pause] And had run on the little hills behind the houses, And pulled small flowers; [pause] But she rose up and went into a strange country, With strange thrones and powers. [short pause] And there were giants in the land she walked in, Tall as their toppling towns. [pause] With heads so high in heaven, the constellations, Served them for crowns; [short pause] And their feet might have forded like a brook the abysses, Where Babel drowns. [pause] And moving in innocence and in accident, She turned the face [short pause] That none has ever looked on without loving, On the Lords of Space; [pause] And One hailed her with her name in our own country, That is full of grace. [long pause] [softly, with wonder] Our Lady went into a strange country, And they crowned her for a queen, [pause] For she needed never to be stayed or questioned, But only seen; [short pause] And they were broken down under unbearable beauty, As we have been. [long pause] [slowly, rising] But ever she walked till away in the last high places, One great light shone [short pause] From the pillared throne of the king of all that country, Who sat thereon; [pause] [hushed, moved] And she cried aloud as she cried under the gibbet, For she saw her Son. [long pause] [tender, heartfelt] Our Lady wears a crown in a strange country, The crown He gave, [pause] But she has not forgotten to call to her old companions, To call and crave; [pause] [quiet, rising hope] And to hear her calling a man might arise and thunder, On the doors of the grave.
