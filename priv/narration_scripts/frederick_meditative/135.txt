Our Lady went into a strange country, 
Our Lady, for she was ours 

And had run on the little hills behind the houses 
And pulled small flowers; 

[thoughtful] But she rose up and went into a strange country 
With strange thrones and powers. 

[short pause] And there were giants in the land she walked in, 
Tall as their toppling towns. 

With heads so high in heaven, the constellations 
Served them for crowns; 

And their feet might have forded like a brook the abysses 
Where Babel drowns. 

[softly] [with quiet wonder] And moving in innocence and in accident, 
She turned the face 

That none has ever looked on without loving 
On the Lords of Space; 

And One hailed her with her name in our own country 
That is full of grace. [long pause]

[gently] [reverently] Our Lady went into a strange country 
And they crowned her for a queen, 

For she needed never to be stayed or questioned 
But only seen; 

[short pause] And they were broken down under unbearable beauty 
As we have been. 

[pause] But ever she walked till away in the last high places 
One great light shone 

From the pillared throne of the king of all that country 
Who sat thereon; 

[softly] [tenderly] And she cried aloud as she cried under the gibbet 
For she saw her Son. [long pause] 

[softly] [warmly] Our Lady wears a crown in a strange country, 
The crown He gave, 

But she has not forgotten to call to her old companions, 
To call and crave; 

[short pause] And to hear her calling a man might arise and thunder 
On the doors of the grave.
